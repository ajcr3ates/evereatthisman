History

Nobody remembers cooking him. Nobody admits to drawing him. Yet every morning, somewhere in the world, a person looks down at breakfast and he looks back up, smiling under that big black hat.

The first recorded sighting came from a small café kitchen. A cook stirred a pot of porridge and found a face settling into the top of it: round cheeks, bright eyes, a wide friendly grin, and a hat. She showed the regulars, took a photo, and served it anyway. The customer said it was the best bowl he had ever eaten and that he felt watched until lunchtime.

Within weeks, similar reports arrived from people who had never met. The face appeared in toast, the foam of cappuccinos, the char of flatbreads and the ridges of waffles. Same cheeks. Same hat. Same grin. Almost everyone who reported him did the same thing next: they ate him!

“I didn’t want to waste it. He looked like he wanted to be eaten.”
Message board post, anonymous

Timeline of sightings

  1. The first bowl. A café cook photographs the face in a pot of porridge. The picture gets passed around the regulars and later posted online.
  2. The toast wave. Dozens of reports of the face in toaster scorch marks, in kitchens on three continents, all in the same month.
  3. Latte art. Baristas report pouring him by accident. Several say they cannot pour anything else for the rest of the shift.
  4. This website. Ever Eat This Man? opens to collect the sightings and ask the obvious question: why does he keep turning up at breakfast?

Theories

Pareidolia. Your brain finds faces in any pattern of light and dark, and breakfast is full of them. This explains a face. It does not explain why it is always the same one, with the same hat.

The collective breakfast. Some visitors think he is a shared idea, passed from bowl to bowl the way a tune gets stuck in many heads at once.

He wants to be eaten. The most popular theory on the board, and the least scientific.
